If I could stand before a thousand faces,
I would not tell them how to win.
I would tell them to stop running
from the sound of their own skin.

I would say: there is a hurt
that learns to act polite.
It answers every message.
It folds itself at night.

It smiles in crowded rooms.
It says, I am almost there.
But somewhere under all that speed,
a small voice asks for air.

Slow down.

Not because the world is gentle.
Not because time will wait.
Slow down because your tired heart
has been standing at the gate.

It has watched you choose the easy yes,
the laugh you did not mean,
the dream you packed away
so no one else would see.

Fear is not always loud.
Sometimes fear is clean.
It keeps a perfect calendar.
It makes a life look green.

But inside, something bends.

A child you used to be
sits quiet in the room.
She still has dirt on both her knees.
She still believes in bloom.

She does not want a trophy.
She does not want applause.
She wants you to remember
what you buried and what it cost.

So when the world says hurry,
put one hand on your chest.
Ask what part of you is tired.
Ask what part has never slept.

Ask what no became yes.
Ask what dream became small.
Ask whose voice you carry
when you answer every call.

There is no shame in shaking.
There is no shame in need.
Even the strongest people
have places that still bleed.

Maybe that is the advice:
do not disappear politely.
Do not become useful
and call it being free.

Come back to yourself slowly.
Come back without a show.
The door was never locked.
You were just afraid to know.

And if you cry, let it happen.
If your hands shake, let them speak.
The heart is not weak for breaking.
It is weak from being meek.

Listen.

Your life is still here,
waiting beneath the noise,
not asking you to be perfect,
only asking for your voice.

So slow down before you vanish.
Let the false hurry pass through.
The person you keep saving
might need to be you.
