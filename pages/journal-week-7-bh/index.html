Do not run so fast from your own name,
do not let the clocks call you tame,
do not trade your small, true fire
for a ladder leaning nowhere higher.

Slow down, I’d say to the rushing crowd,
to the busy heads and the hearts too loud,
to the ones who smile when they want to cry,
to the ones who say yes with a no nearby.

There is a hurry that looks like grace.
It combs its hair. It washes its face.
It says, be good, be quick, be kind,
then steals the chairs from inside your mind.

It says, answer now.
It says, don’t wait.
It says, call your fear
a cleaner fate.

And off you go in your polished shoes,
carrying everybody else’s news,
folding your dreams till they fit in a drawer,
then wondering what the drawer was for.

I have seen a person vanish slow,
not with thunder, not with show,
but one small yes, and one small lie,
and one more morning passing by.

The mouth says, fine.
The hands say, sure.
The body says,
I can’t do more.

Listen there.

Listen under the tidy word,
under the thing you almost heard,
under the laugh you made too bright,
under the sleep that comes too light.

There is a self you left behind
in the back room of your busy mind.
It does not shout. It does not plead.
It only holds the thing you need.

Maybe it is no.
Maybe it is stay.
Maybe it is go.
Maybe not today.

Maybe it is hunger with its shoes untied.
Maybe it is anger you taught to hide.
Maybe it is grief in a plain gray coat,
sitting there with a stone in its throat.

Do not go fast from that quiet room.
Do not call your silence bloom.
Do not call it peace because nobody heard
the breaking bone of the swallowed word.

If I had one thing to tell them all,
the tall, the tired, the proud, the small,
I’d say: stand still before you choose.
Some roads win because people lose.

The world will clap for the useful you.
It will hand you a list and a costume too.
It will praise your pace and count your miles,
then borrow your face and sell your smiles.

But you are not made of done and due.
You are not a task the world got through.
You are not a bell for others to ring,
not a pocket, a ladder, a folded thing.

Say: this fear is mine.
Say: this want is true.
Say: this tired life
still belongs to you.

Say it plain.
Say it late.
Say it with your hand
on your own heart’s gate.

For time is sharp and time is sly.
It takes the years we toss it by.
It takes the yes we did not mean.
It takes the dreams we kept too clean.

But still, the self can stumble back,
barefoot down the old cracked track,
with dust in its hair and truth in its hand,
asking only that you understand.

So slow down, friend, before you start.
Put your ear to your stubborn heart.
Let the loud world spin and shout.

The life you save
may be the one
you were living
without.
