The city has curled itself
into a black little planet.
Buildings lean like questions.
The street keeps turning.

Every car goes around
the same hard center.
Every window looks awake.
Nobody looks rested.

This is what hurry does.

It bends the road under your feet.
It makes the sky feel low.
It teaches your hands to move
before your heart can know.

I would tell the crowd:
look at the buildings.
Even steel can seem afraid
when the world spins too fast.

Look at the road,
folded into a circle.
That is how a life feels
when every day is only next.

You wake. You answer.
You rush. You agree.
You become useful.
You become hard to see.

And somewhere inside you,
smaller than a window light,
a quiet self keeps waving
from the edge of the night.

It does not hate you.
It has waited too long for hate.
It just wants you to stop
before you call this fate.

Slow down.

Not softly.
Not sweetly.
Slow down like a person
stepping back from a ledge.

The city will keep spinning.
The lights will keep their teeth.
The street will keep pretending
there is no ground beneath.

But you are not the traffic.
You are not the glass.
You are not the blur
that everyone lets pass.

You are the breath
before the answer.
You are the ache
before the yes.

You are the small hand
knocking from inside your chest,
saying, please,
come back before there is less.

So if I had one thing
to give a crowded room,
I would not give a map.
I would give them permission.

Permission to stop.
Permission to shake.
Permission to admit
the brave face can break.

Permission to stand still
while the city rolls by,
to feel the old fear rise,
and not make it a lie.

Because the world can look endless
and still make you small.
It can fill every street
and leave no room at all.

Listen.

Under the engine.
Under the rain.
Under the beautiful panic
pretending it is plain.

Your life is calling
from the center of the spin.
Not asking you to escape,
only asking to be let in.

So slow down before you disappear
into the city's turning face.
The whole world can move around you.
You still deserve a place.
