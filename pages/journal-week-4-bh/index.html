Journal Week 4

The Chicken Has Notes

I would like to do more laughing at small things, because my brain can turn a regular Tuesday into a board meeting with thunder. A chicken does not live like that. She enters the yard like she owns the weather, scratches the dirt with professional confidence, and announces every crumb as if she discovered gold. I respect that level of drama. If I dropped a tortilla chip and screamed about it, people would worry, but the chicken makes it look like purpose. Maybe I need more of that: less panic, more strut. More looking ridiculous without filing a report against myself.

The steps are not complicated, which is rude because I prefer my problems with special effects. I can take one walk without bringing every unfinished task with me. I can make something ugly on purpose and let it exist for ten whole minutes. I can stop treating rest like a crime scene. I can put my phone down, drink my coffee while it is still warm, and let the day cluck at me instead of chase me. I do not need to become a chicken. I only need to borrow her courage: chest out, eyes bright, absolutely convinced the world still has snacks hidden somewhere.
